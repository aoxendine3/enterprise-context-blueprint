## 2. The Phonetic Pronunciation Law for "Live"

In standard English, the word **"live"** is a heteronym with two distinct pronunciations depending on syntactic category:

| Usage Category | Part of Speech | IPA Phonetic | Rhymes With | Example Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operational Broadcast / Stream** | Adjective / Adverb | **/laɪv/** (Long 'i') | *alive, hive, five* | *"live broadcast", "live recording", "live telemetry", "going live"* |
| **Residency / Biological Existence** | Verb | **/lɪv/** (Short 'i') | *give, sieve* | *"where we live", "live and let live"* |

### Canonical Rule:
In XORAS operations, our usage is almost exclusively the adjective/adverb. Standard Text-to-Speech models frequently default to the verb pronunciation (`/lɪv/`), producing an awkward "liv stream". 
* **Enforced Normalization:** In all agent speech pipelines, the word "live" when modifying broadcasts, streams, sessions, telemetry, audio, or stages is phonetically remapped to **`lyve`** or **`/laɪv/`** to guarantee the crisp long 'i' sound.

### Financial Terminology Rules:
1. **Never read currency symbols as isolated words:** Never say *"dollar one point five million"*; always say *"one point five million dollars"*.
2. **Parenthetical Accounting Figures:** Financial reports represent negative cash flows in parentheses (e.g., `($12.4M)`). The engine must verbalize this as *"a net loss of twelve point four million dollars"* or *"negative twelve point four million dollars"*, never *"open parenthesis twelve point four million dollars close parenthesis"*.
